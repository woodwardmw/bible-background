# 2 John - Background & Links

![A single sheet of papyrus with a reed pen and inkwell on a plain wooden table](../images/2-john-1.webp)

*One sheet of papyrus, a reed pen and lamp-black ink - "I would rather not use paper and ink" (v12).*

**Passage snapshot**  
Thirteen verses, the length of a single sheet of papyrus, from "the elder" to a congregation addressed as a lady and her children. He is glad some of them are walking in the truth, repeats the old command to love one another, warns that deceivers denying Jesus Christ come in the flesh are circulating, and tells the congregation not to house them or greet them. Then he stops, because he would rather say the rest in person.

## Map of the text
- vv1-3: The elder to the elect lady and her children, whom he loves in truth, along with all who know the truth; grace, mercy and peace from the Father and from Jesus Christ the Son of the Father, in truth and love.
- v4: Joy at finding some of her children walking in the truth, as commanded by the Father.
- vv5-6: Not a new commandment but the one held from the beginning - that we love one another; and love is defined as walking according to his commandments.
- v7: Many deceivers have gone out into the world, who do not confess Jesus Christ coming in the flesh. Such a one is the deceiver and the antichrist.
- vv8-9: Watch yourselves, so as not to lose what was worked for but to receive a full reward; anyone who runs ahead and does not remain in the teaching of Christ does not have God, while the one who remains in it has both Father and Son.
- vv10-11: If anyone comes to you and does not bring this teaching, do not receive him into the house or greet him; the one who greets him shares in his evil works.
- vv12-13: Much more to write, but not with paper and ink; a hope to come and speak face to face, so that joy may be complete. Greetings from the children of your elect sister.

## Historical background (what a first-century hearer would notice)
- The letter fits the standard conventions of a short Greco-Roman letter, and it fits them because it fits a single sheet of papyrus. The abrupt "much more to write, but not with paper and ink" (v12) is partly a courtesy and partly a description of the writer running out of room.
- "The elder" identifies the sender by office and reputation rather than name, which only works if the recipients need no more than that. Early tradition connects the Johannine letters with Ephesus and Asia Minor, and Papias refers to a "John the elder" whom later writers sometimes distinguished from the apostle.
- "The elect lady and her children" is a congregation, not a woman. The clincher is v13, where greetings come from "the children of your elect sister" - the sending congregation, described the same way. Compare Peter's "she who is at Babylon, chosen together with you" (1 Pet 5:13).
- The deceivers do not confess Jesus Christ *erchomenon en sarki*, "coming in the flesh", using a present participle where 1 John 4:2 uses a perfect. The difference is small and the target is not: teaching that treated the incarnation as an appearance rather than a permanent reality, a strand of thought that hardened into full docetism in the following century.
- The word behind "runs ahead" in v9 is *proagon*, to go out in front. It sounds like a boast the teachers made for themselves - progress, advancement beyond the elementary teaching - and the elder turns it into the charge against them. Advancing past the incarnation means advancing out of Christianity.
- Hospitality was the entire infrastructure of the itinerant mission. Congregations met in houses, travelling teachers depended on being taken in, and a host's welcome was a public endorsement of the guest. Refusing the house and the greeting is not personal rudeness; it is denying a teacher a platform and a supply line. The Didache, written not long after, deals with the same problem by setting limits on how long an itinerant may stay and what he may ask for.
- The greeting itself carried weight. To greet a visiting teacher in front of the assembly was to vouch for him, which is why v11 can say the one who greets him shares in his works.

## Audience needs (why this mattered to them)
- This community had already been through a split. 1 John 2:19 describes people who "went out from us but were not of us", and 2 John shows the aftermath: the group that left is now travelling, and it is calling.
- A congregation that had been taught to love one another needed to know how that command interacts with a teacher who denies the incarnation. The elder's answer is that love does not require an open door, because love was defined at the start as walking in the commandments, not as unlimited accommodation.
- Small house congregations had no structures for vetting visitors. The letter gives them a single, portable test - what does he say about Jesus Christ come in the flesh - which an ordinary household could apply without an apostle present.
- v8's warning about losing "what we have worked for" speaks to a real fear that a congregation could be talked out of what it had been given. The elder does not treat that as unthinkable.

## Canonical placement
- New covenant era, late first century, in the second-generation life of congregations whose founding figures are dying and whose doctrine is being contested from inside.
- The letter belongs to a Johannine cluster: the Gospel's "walk in the light" and "abide in me", 1 John's tests of true confession, and 3 John's problem of hospitality abused from the other direction, where Diotrephes refuses to welcome legitimate brothers (3 John 9-10). Read side by side, 2 and 3 John are the two failures of a single instinct.
- Along with 3 John, this was among the books Eusebius listed as disputed in the early church, which says less about its content than about how easily a private note to one congregation could go unnoticed.

## Scripture links
- The command from the beginning: John 13:34-35; 1 John 2:7-8.
- Confessing Christ come in the flesh: 1 John 4:2-3, with the perfect participle; and John 1:14.
- Those who went out from us: 1 John 2:19.
- The church addressed as a woman: 1 Pet 5:13; Eph 5:25-27; Rev 19:7-8.
- Testing itinerant teachers: Matt 7:15-20; 1 Thess 5:21; and the hospitality instructions of Matt 10:11-14.
- Hospitality rightly given to travelling workers: 3 John 5-8, the mirror image of vv10-11.
- Face to face rather than by letter: Rom 1:11-12; 3 John 13-14, which closes with nearly the same words.

## Message to the first hearers
- Truth and love are not competing pressures to be balanced. The letter uses both words repeatedly and treats them as the same thing viewed from two sides: love is walking in the commandments, and truth is what love walks in.
- The confession is not negotiable, and the reason is that it concerns whether God actually came. A teaching that makes the flesh of Jesus temporary or apparent is not a variation; it removes the ground the congregation is standing on.
- Discernment is a congregational duty, not a specialist one. The elder hands the test to a household and expects it to be used at the door.
- The closing is the point of the whole letter. He would rather not do this in writing at all; what he wants is to be there, and for their joy to be complete. The severity of vv10-11 is in service of that.
