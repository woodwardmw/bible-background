## The thread

Isaiah 26 and 1 Peter 5 both hand their readers the same short phrase, and it does the same work in each.

Isaiah tells a people in the middle of judgement to "enter your chambers... hide yourselves for **a little while** until the fury has passed" (26:20). Peter tells scattered congregations that "after you have suffered **a little while**, the God of all grace... will himself restore, confirm, strengthen and establish you" (5:10). Neither is denying that the pressure is real. Both are making a claim about its duration, and asking for a posture in the meantime: Isaiah's waiting behind a shut door, Peter's humbling under a hand that lifts at a time you do not choose.

They also share the admission underneath it. Isaiah 26:18 is one of the most honest verses in the prophets - "we were with child, we writhed, but we gave birth to wind" - a people conceding they produced no deliverance at all. Peter's answer to the same helplessness is to stop trying to produce it: cast the anxiety, put on the apron, wait for the lifting.

Judges 16 does not share that posture, and putting it alongside the other two is instructive precisely because it does not. Samson is the man who never humbles himself under anyone's hand. His strength departs without his noticing (16:20), and his last prayer is still about his own two eyes rather than his people. Where Isaiah's Judah says plainly that it gave birth to wind, Samson goes out "as at other times" and assumes the strength is his. Read together, the two Old Testament chapters are the same failure met with opposite responses.
