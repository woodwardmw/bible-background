## The thread

Isaiah 25 and 1 Peter 4 sit unusually close together today, on two words.

The first is **reproach**. Isaiah promises that God "will take away the reproach of his people from all the earth" (25:8), spoken to a nation whose humiliation was public and whose standing could not be recovered by its own effort. Peter writes to people carrying reproach right now, insulted for the name, and tells them it is the mark of blessing rather than abandonment: "the Spirit of glory and of God rests upon you" (4:14). One text promises the shame will be lifted; the other says what to do while it is still on you.

The second is **judgement, and where it starts**. Isaiah's hymn of rescue does not end in rescue. It closes on Moab trodden into a dung pit (25:10-12), refusing to let comfort become complacency. Peter puts the same edge on his own encouragement: "judgement begins at the household of God" (4:17). Both are speaking to people who have every reason to think of themselves as the wronged party, and both insist that being wronged is not the same as being exempt.

Judges 15 does not belong to that conversation, and it would be a stretch to force it in. It contributes something different: a picture of the problem those promises answer. Judah fields three thousand men to bind its own deliverer, explaining that the Philistines rule over them (15:11), and the reproach there is not imposed from outside at all. It is chosen.
