## The thread

Two of today's three chapters reach for the same event from opposite ends.

Isaiah 24 charges the whole earth with breaking "the everlasting covenant" (24:5) - not Sinai, which was Israel's alone, but the covenant God made with all flesh after the flood (Gen 9:16). It then opens "the windows of heaven" (24:18), lifting the phrase straight out of the flood narrative (Gen 7:11). The flood is Isaiah's picture of judgement falling on everyone.

1 Peter 3 takes the same waters and reads them as rescue: eight people brought safely through, and baptism as the corresponding reality (3:20-21). For a scattered minority in Asia Minor, the reassuring detail is the number. Eight.

One event, judgement and salvation held together. Peter is not softening Isaiah; he is standing on the far side of the same water.

Judges 14 runs on a different track, but it shares Isaiah's diagnosis at a personal scale. Samson is consecrated from the womb and spends that consecration piece by piece, and the book's verdict on his era - everyone doing what was right in his own eyes - is what Isaiah 24:5 says of the whole earth.
